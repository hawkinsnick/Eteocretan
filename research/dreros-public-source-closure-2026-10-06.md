# Dreros public-source closure pass — 2026-10-06

## Dreros 1 / Isaluria route

The original Van Effenterre 1946 *Revue de Philologie* article remains the preferred primary publication target (vol. 20, pp. 131–138).

A lawful public derivative critical witness is available:
Michel Lejeune, “L'inscription Isaluria de Drèros : étéocrétois ou crétois ?”, *Revue des Études Anciennes* 49 (1947), pp. 274–285, Persée DOI 10.3406/rea.1947.5361.

Lejeune explicitly identifies Van Effenterre's 1946 article, reports that it contained a facsimile, describes the inscription as five lines with the first two retrograde and the last three boustrophedon, and records uncertainty in several readings. This independently supports the project's policy that the secondary/derivative witness must not be flattened into a clean canonical transcription.

**Disposition:** Lejeune is admitted as a source-lineage/uncertainty witness, not as a replacement for direct primary collation and not as independent observation of the stone.

## Physical-object whereabouts

Public sources disagree over whether relevant Dreros stones were kept at Neapolis, transferred, or lost. No authoritative current museum inventory record was found in this pass for the Eteocretan stones. Preserve `current_location = unresolved`; do not infer Heraklion or Neapolis accession numbers from general statements about the excavation.
